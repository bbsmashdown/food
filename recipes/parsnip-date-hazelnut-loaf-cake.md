## Ingredients

- 1 | tbsp | butter | dairy
- 1 1/4 | cup | all-purpose flour | dry
- 2 | tbsp | lemon juice | produce
- 1 | cup | powdered sugar | dry
- 2 | tsp | lemon zest | produce
- 1/8 | tsp | black pepper | dry
- 8 | oz | parsnips | produce
- 1 | tsp | baking soda | dry
- 1 | tsp | kosher salt | dry
- 1/2 | cup | chopped dates | produce
- 2 | | eggs | dairy
- 3/4 | cup | granulated sugar | dry
- 1/4 | cup | dark brown sugar | dry
- 1 | tsp | vanilla extract | dry
- 2/3 | cup | extra-virgin olive oil | dry
- 3/4 | cup | hazelnuts | dry

## Steps

1. Heat the oven to 325°F. Generously butter an 8 x 5-inch loaf pan. Add a few spoonfuls of flour, shake to coat all the surfaces, and tap out any excess. Toast and finely chop the hazelnuts.
2. Make the glaze: stir together the lemon juice and powdered sugar in a bowl. Stir in 1 teaspoon of the lemon zest and several twists of pepper. Set aside.
3. Put the parsnips (cut into 1-inch pieces) in a food processor and process until finely chopped, like the texture of couscous. You want about 1 1/2 cups. If you don't have a processor, grate them. Return them to the processor if you took them out to measure.
4. Whisk together the flour, baking soda and salt in a medium bowl. Add the dates and toss until coated with flour so they don't clump.
5. To the food processor, add the eggs, granulated sugar, brown sugar, vanilla and the remaining 1 teaspoon of lemon zest. Pulse until smooth and well blended. Pulse in the olive oil to make a creamy consistency.
6. Pour the parsnip mixture into the dry ingredients and fold until blended. Add the hazelnuts and fold until there are no more bits of flour.
7. Pour the batter into the prepared pan. Bake until slightly risen and a pick or thin knife blade inserted into the center comes out clean, 35 to 45 minutes for a metal pan, longer for glass or ceramic.
8. Cool the cake in the pan for 10 minutes, then run a knife around the edges to release it. Set the warm cake on a rack over a tray to catch the glaze.
9. Drizzle the glaze all over the top while the cake is still warm. To serve as a breakfast bread, leave off the glaze.
