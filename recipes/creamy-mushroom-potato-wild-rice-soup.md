## Ingredients

- 2/3 | cup | wild rice | dry
- 2 | tsp | kosher salt | dry
- 2 | tbsp | olive oil | dry
- 1 | lb | mixed mushrooms | produce
- 1/4 | tsp | dried chile flakes | dry
- 1/2 | tsp | black pepper | dry
- 4 | tbsp | unsalted butter | dairy
- 1 | | leek | produce
- 3 | clove | garlic | produce
- 3 | tbsp | tomato paste | dry
- 1 | tbsp | smoked paprika | dry
- 1/4 | tsp | caraway seeds | dry
- 4 | cup | chicken or vegetable broth | dry
- 1 | | Yukon Gold potato | produce
- 1 | handful | thyme | produce
- 2 | | rosemary sprigs | produce
- 1 | cup | heavy cream | dairy
- 1/4 | cup | fresh dill | produce

## Steps

1. Place the wild rice in a medium saucepan with 1 teaspoon salt and water to cover by about 3 inches. Bring to a boil, reduce to a lively simmer, cover and cook until the rice is fully tender and most of the grains have opened, about 45 minutes. Check occasionally that the water hasn't cooked off. Drain thoroughly and set aside.
2. Pour a generous glug of olive oil into a large skillet or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the mushrooms in an even layer (in batches if needed) and season with the chile flakes and a sprinkling of salt and pepper. Don't disturb them until nicely browned on one side, then flip and cook until browned and slightly crisp at the edges, about 10 minutes per batch. If they give off a lot of liquid, keep cooking until it evaporates and the mushrooms brown.
3. Scrape the mushrooms into a bowl and set aside. Return the pan to the heat, reduce to medium, and add the butter and leek with a light seasoning of salt and pepper. Cook about 10 minutes, until the leek is soft and fragrant but not browned. Add the garlic and cook, stirring, another minute or two. Add the tomato paste, smoked paprika and caraway seeds.
4. Cook, stirring and scraping the pan bottom, until the tomato paste has darkened and thickened a bit, about 5 minutes.
5. Add the broth, potato, thyme and rosemary. Bring to a simmer and cook until the potato pieces are tender and easily crushed with a fork, about 20 minutes.
6. Add the cream, the drained wild rice and the cooked mushrooms. Simmer gently until the soup has thickened a bit and the raw cream flavor has cooked off, another 15 minutes or so. If the soup is getting very thick, add a bit of broth or water. It should be rich and creamy but not gloppy.
7. Taste and adjust the seasoning with more salt, pepper or chile flakes. If you can, fish out and discard the thyme and rosemary stems, then add the dill. Serve hot.
8. Leftovers keep in the refrigerator for up to 2 days. Don't freeze this one, because the mushrooms can develop a weird texture.
